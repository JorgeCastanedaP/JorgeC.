# HR Analytics Case: Attrition in a Firm

As part of a team project for my Business Programming course, I worked on a real-world case study for a fictional large company, XYZ, which faces an annual attrition rate of about 15%—affecting roughly 600 employees each year. Our goal was to help the company identify the key drivers of employee attrition and recommend actionable insights to improve retention.

Using a logistic regression model, we analyzed employee data to predict the probability of attrition and determine which workplace factors most significantly impact employee turnover. I was responsible for data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, and creating clear visualizations to support our findings.

Throughout the project, we focused on:

* Understanding the dataset and its variables

* Defining a clear analytical problem

* Cleaning and transforming the data

* Conducting thorough exploratory data analysis (EDA)

* Building and interpreting a logistic regression model

* Presenting actionable insights to management

This experience strengthened my skills in data-driven storytelling, model interpretation, and collaborative problem-solving in a business context.

# Fintech and Crowdfunding: Kickstarter

For the final project in my FinTech course, I worked in a team to explore the Crowdfunding sector, with a specific focus on Kickstarter. Our objective was to analyze how this emerging financial model has disrupted traditional funding methods and to assess its broader impact on the financial ecosystem.

We began by breaking down the structure of crowdfunding platforms and identifying key pain points faced by both creators and backers, such as project credibility, fulfillment risk, and payment processing challenges. We then examined the innovative solutions offered by platforms like Kickstarter to address these issues, including tiered rewards, funding thresholds, and integrated payment systems.

Additionally, we evaluated how traditional financial institutions have responded to the rise of crowdfunding—whether through collaboration, competition, or adaptation.

This project gave me a strong understanding of how financial innovation can democratize access to capital, and it deepened my appreciation for the evolving relationship between fintech platforms and legacy finance.

# Investments: Portfolio Simulation

For a semester-long project in an Investments course, I partnered with my teammate Andrew Cash to manage a $1 million portfolio through Stocktrak, with the goal of outperforming the market. Our strategy involved analyzing and making investment decisions based on a variety of financial metrics, including Sharpe ratio, beta, and alpha. These metrics allowed us to track risk-adjusted returns, assess market volatility, and measure how well our portfolio performed relative to the market.

Throughout the project, we applied both technical and fundamental analysis to select stocks and build a diversified portfolio. The key challenge was to make strategic decisions under a time constraint while ensuring our portfolio was resilient to market fluctuations. By the end of the semester, we evaluated our performance using these metrics and compared our results to broader market benchmarks.

This hands-on project helped me strengthen my skills in portfolio management, financial analysis, and investment strategy.
